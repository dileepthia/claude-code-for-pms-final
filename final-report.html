The one-minute truth

On 12 August, Release 4.2 turned two dials at once — and the floor fell out.

For weeks everyone called it “a slow August.” It wasn’t. Here is exactly what the release did.

Dial I · The Clock

90 seconds → 60 seconds

The time a phone rings before the call moves on was cut by a third. Reliable people reached for the phone — and it was already gone.

Dial II · The Favouritism

Stack calls on the nearest

Routing was re-weighted toward proximity, piling each area’s calls onto one favoured local — and freezing out everyone else nearby.

77%→54%
acceptance, the week 4.2 shipped
2%→~21%
missed calls
🐶

For a five-year-old: the phone that calls the heroes started hanging up too fast, and it kept calling the same favourite hero over and over — so the other heroes stopped getting calls at all, and sat by a quiet phone wondering what they did wrong.

And the cruelty of it: the system scores a missed call exactly like a refusal, and never forgives. So the frozen-out responders couldn’t climb back on their own. Four of them went completely dark.

I Module 1 · Origin Story
The Contract

A new PM, two weeks on the job, handed a mess he wasn’t in the room for. First move: trust no document on its face. Cross-examine every source against every other.

The finding

The release notes said one thing, a product brief said the opposite about “bulk callout.” The instinct: trust the code, not the paperwork.

The twist the investigation turned up: the routing code can’t even settle it — that service only decides the order people get asked, not fan-out. Three independent records say it shipped; only one stale brief says it didn’t. So the brief is the out-of-date page — not the release notes running ahead. One question to the Gunsmith closes it.

The prompt that cracked it

for bulk callout, shouldn't we rely on the codebase as opposed to the document? Maybe the release notes was updated and changes pushed out in error and ahead of the actual code being deployed?
01-origin-story · a source-of-truth instinct
II Module 2 · Super Hearing
The Whispers

Listen to everyone — four handlers on recorded calls, 147 tickets from the street. Then refuse to be fooled by the loudest number in the room.

The finding

A number was going around: “pings are down almost 50% since 4.2.” It looked like the system had gone quiet.

It was a mirage. Pings per week never moved — flat at 158–177 the entire time. The “before” bucket just held 6.3 weeks and the “after” held 3.7. Fewer weeks, not fewer pings. The calls hadn’t vanished — they’d been redistributed. The real story was hiding behind a misleading headline.

The prompt that cracked it

Excellent, now after 4.2, the total number of pings itself has reduced by almost 50%. Is there data to explain this
02-super-hearing · don’t trust the headline number
III Module 3 · Rewind
The Reckoning

Rewind the tape on one name. Farlight’s phone went silent — so follow her calls, week by week, and find out who got them instead.

The finding

Farlight wasn’t sent farther afield. Her own backyard was handed to someone else.

Her own-area pings fell 59 → 6 (−90%). She never worked out of area — 0 of 16 far pings before, 0 of 5 after — so she had no fallback. Meanwhile The Gale, an identical homebody one area over, gained +20% a week. Same kind of person, opposite fate. It was never homebody-vs-traveller — it was winner-vs-loser inside an area.

The prompt that cracked it

After 10 Aug there was a significant drop of pings for Farlight. Can you check if Farlight received near or farther away pings before and after 10 Aug and how many before and after
03-rewind · follow one person all the way down
IV Module 4 · X-Ray Vision
The Rules That Broke

Why can’t the dark responders recover? “Bad luck,” someone said. Not good enough. Read the actual routing code and name the trap.

The finding — the score trap

In the code, a responder’s score only goes up when they take a job.

It goes down for a miss and a refusal — scored identically — and there’s no forgiveness over time (the fix was left as a TODO since 2019). So a starved responder gets no calls → can’t take → can’t climb. A one-way door. Not luck — a trap. The reset: let a quiet score heal over time, stop counting a miss as a “no,” and hand out a few guaranteed at-bats.

The prompt that cracked it

I don't believe in luck. What else can we do to reset so these responders get a chance to start increasing the score? Obviously they can't move locations so don't suggest that
04-x-ray-vision · reject the easy answer, constrain the fix
+ take
only way the score rises
− miss = − no
a timeout punished like a refusal
∞ no decay
the score never heals

No calls → no takes → no points up → fewer calls. The one-way door.

V Module 5 · Super Speed
The Weapon

Winston’s rule: don’t fix it quietly in the dark — build the part a person can see, and prove it works. So we built “Back in rotation” — and here it is, fully loaded. Not a screenshot. The real thing.

What it does

When a responder has had no calls offered for 3 days, the system re-inserts them automatically, gives the next call a longer window, and stops counting a miss against them. The handler sees a flag — “being passed over, not turning jobs down” — and the responder just sees an ambient “Actively receiving pings.”

Live model  — move the levers, scrub the 10-week timeline, then flip “Back in rotation.” Watch the quiet count drop from 3 → 0 and four people come back.

The prompt that built it

Show a professional layout

VI Module 6 · Sidekicks
The Rules of the Continental

“Rules. Without them, we live with the animals.” The last move isn’t a fix — it’s a guard that never sleeps. A skill you point at any brief, so a half-baked one never ships blind again.

The four gates

Every one of the briefs that caused trouble failed exactly one of these. The skill — /review-checklist — checks all four and flags what’s missing, pointing to where.

The prompt that forged it

Make a skill called review-checklist. When I point it at a brief, it checks that the brief: names who owns it, says how we'll know it worked, keeps the same scope start to finish, and explains the problem before the fix.
06-sidekicks · build the guard, don’t be the guard

Owner named

II

States how we’ll know it worked — a number, a target, an outcome.

III

IV

The High Table · the decision

Ship the two config fixes now. Build “Back in rotation” — the part a person can see. And measure recovery the right way.

  • ✓
    The four dark responders (Vesper, Farlight, The Undertow, Meteor Mite) get offered calls again and their weekly takes recover.
  • ✓
    Drive the missed-call rate back toward its ~2% baseline — not just the headline acceptance number.
  • ✓
    Watch acceptance & missed, not ticket volume — tickets lag the real picture by about two weeks.
  • ✓
    No responder sits quiet longer than ~2 weeks without being offered work or a handler being shown it.
One decision for the table
Approve the scope — and the call that re-insertion is automatic, not another button for a handler to press.
